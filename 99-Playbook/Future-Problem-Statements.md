# Future problem statements

## 1. Fragmented health information across systems
Consumers see incomplete views across payers, providers, and records. This causes repeated paperwork and inconsistent recommendations.

## 2. Poor provider choice and switching friction
Consumers struggle to compare providers on price, network fit, and review quality; switching is often the only escape route.

## 3. Delayed access and wait times
Access is often constrained by staffing and scheduling, leading to postponed care and service loss.

## 4. High-deductible / medical-debt burden
Patients with employer-sponsored HDHPs face large upfront out-of-pocket costs, contributing to debt and skipped care.

## 5. Prior-authorization friction
Authorization rules create uncertainty and administrative overhead before treatment decisions.

## 6. Care navigation and handoffs
Incomplete care pathways create confusion across specialists, urgent care, and lab or imaging links.

## 7. Low digital self-service
Patients need one-place digital tools rather than repeated calls or fragmented portals.

## 8. Consumer cost transparency gap
Patients don't get reliable out-of-pocket cost estimates before choosing care. This is the main active scenario.
