# Project-to-Product

Use when the organization funds and staffs work as projects, and that model blocks durable product
teams. The transition changes **product taxonomy, team topology, funding, flow metrics, and
governance** — it is not a product lifecycle stage.

## Flow

| Stage | Question | Artifact |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio inventory | What projects, products, and platforms exist now? | Project and product inventory |
| Product taxonomy | Which durable products and platforms should exist? | Product taxonomy |
| Team topology | Which teams own which product outcomes? | Team topology |
| Funding model | How are products funded across horizons instead of project-by-project? | Product funding model |
| Flow metrics | Where is work waiting, and how efficiently does it flow? | Flow metrics baseline |
| Governance | What decisions move from project gates to product reviews? | Product governance model |
| Transition roadmap | What changes first, and what proves the transition is working? | Transition roadmap |
| Review | Are teams durable, funded, and improving flow? | Transition review |

## Flow metrics to baseline

| Metric | What it exposes |
|---|---|
| Flow time | Time from customer request to delivered outcome |
| Flow load | Work in progress versus capacity |
| Flow efficiency | Ratio of value-adding time to waiting time |
| Flow distribution | Mix of value work, defects, risks, and chore |

Use the four together. A single velocity number hides the system constraint.

## What good looks like

- Teams are durable and own a product outcome, not a temporary project scope.
- Funding follows products across horizons, not annual project approvals.
- Flow metrics show whether the transition is reducing waiting and rework.
- Governance reviews outcomes and system constraints, not task completion.
