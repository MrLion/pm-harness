# Persona — benefits or payer buyer (unresolved)

> [!WARNING] Hypothesis, not evidence
> The buyer is unresolved in this case. This persona names the possible buyers so the decision can test them; it does not claim any of them has been interviewed.

## Buyer options

| Buyer | What they would buy | Why they might care | What would confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employer benefits lead | A cost-transparency benefit for employees | Lower surprise-bill complaints and better care navigation | Benefits strategy and budget interview |
| Payer product lead | A member-facing estimate experience | Member retention, call deflection, network steering | Product and data-owner interview |
| Provider finance or access lead | A pre-service estimate and reconciliation workflow | Fewer billing surprises, fewer calls, better collections | Revenue-cycle and access interview |

## Jobs

- When a member asks what imaging will cost, I want to give a defensible answer before the service.
- When a patient receives a bill, I want the estimate and the final responsibility to reconcile.
- When I sponsor a tool, I want evidence that it changes a decision or reduces a cost.

## Decision criteria

| Criterion | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Data access and reconciliation | An estimate without final-responsibility data creates a new trust problem |
| Member or patient reach | The buyer needs a channel to the person making the booking decision |
| Compliance and BAA path | Real patient data requires a lawful data relationship |
| Time to evidence | The buyer will not fund a multi-quarter build without a behavioural signal |
| Support and operations | Estimates create questions; someone must own the answer |

## Unknowns

- Who pays for the estimate experience?
- Who owns the estimate data and the reconciliation?
- Is the buyer the same actor as the user?
- Does the channel sit with the provider, payer, or employer?
