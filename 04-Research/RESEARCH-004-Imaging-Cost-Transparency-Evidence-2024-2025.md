# Research — imaging cost knowledge, estimate accuracy, and price variation

## Question this answers

What is known about whether U.S. imaging patients know their out-of-pocket cost before care, how accurate the estimates are, and how much prices vary by site of care?

## Method

Desk research across three sources:

- [Grant et al., *Unintended Consequences of Price Transparency Initiatives: Examining Patient Decision Making in Imaging Services*, JACR, 2024-10-15](https://www.jacr.org/article/S1546-1440(24)00831-7/fulltext) — 532 adults scheduled for or completing outpatient CT, MRI, or PET/CT at a Southern California academic system, November 2022–May 2023. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39950202/).
- [Philips and Whaley, *Commercial price variation for common imaging studies*, Health Affairs Scholar, 2025-04-30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12097484) — Transparency-in-Coverage negotiated rates for 30 common imaging studies, 2023 contract year, four national insurers.
- [Maughan et al., *Commercial Insurers Paid More For Procedures At Hospital Outpatient Departments Than At Ambulatory Surgical Centers*, Health Affairs, 2025-10-06](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/abs/10.1377/hlthaff.2025.00297) — 2024 Transparency-in-Coverage prices for 13 common procedures.

## Findings

- **Finding.** Most patients in the JACR sample did not know their out-of-pocket cost before imaging.
  - Evidence: 15.4% (82 of 532) reported pre-imaging out-of-pocket cost knowledge. Among those aware, the most common source was direct inquiry to the insurer (23.4%); 18.5% knew from benefit design; 17.3% received it from the imaging center without asking.
  - Confidence: medium for this single academic system; low for employer-sponsored HDHP members generally.
- **Finding.** Cost knowledge did not predict whether patients completed the exam.
  - Evidence: the study found no evidence of an association between prospective out-of-pocket cost exposure and appointment cancellation or no-show. In the companion analysis, 21.9% of patients who canceled knew their cost versus 11.9% of those who completed.
  - Confidence: medium for this sample; the result weakens any claim that an estimate alone changes completion.
- **Finding.** Commercial imaging prices vary widely by site, especially facility fees.
  - Evidence: facility-fee variation was 3–6× higher than professional-fee variation across 30 imaging studies. For brain MRI with and without contrast (CPT 70553), the facility fee mean was `$1,210`, median `$792`, IQR `$515–$1,487`.
  - Confidence: medium for negotiated prices; not a measure of patient responsibility.
- **Finding.** Hospital outpatient departments are paid more than ambulatory surgical centers for the same procedures.
  - Evidence: 2024 commercial prices were `$1,489` (78%) higher in hospital outpatient departments than in ambulatory surgical centers across 13 procedures; the differential ranged from `$327` at Cigna to `$1,673` at UnitedHealthcare.
  - Confidence: medium for the commercial price comparison; not a patient-facing estimate.

## Raw signal

> "Overall, 15.4% (82 of 532) of the participants reported having preimaging OOPC knowledge before their appointment." — [JACR](https://www.jacr.org/article/S1546-1440(24)00831-7/fulltext)

> "Facility fee variation was 3-6x higher than physician fees, with major variation by payer and state." — [Philips and Whaley, Health Affairs Scholar](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12097484)

## What this does NOT tell us

- Whether the JACR sample represents employer-sponsored HDHP members; it is one Southern California academic system.
- Whether patients who knew their cost made a better or worse decision; the study found no association with cancellation or no-show.
- Whether an estimate is accurate against final patient responsibility after adjudication. The JACR study measures knowledge, not reconciliation.
- Whether patients can use site-of-care price differences, or whether a lower price would change where they book.
- Whether the price variation produces lower out-of-pocket cost after plan cost-sharing; the data are negotiated allowed amounts.
