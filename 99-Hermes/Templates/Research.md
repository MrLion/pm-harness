# Research — consumer cost transparency in U.S. healthcare

## Question this answers

Do U.S. healthcare consumers receive useful cost estimates before care, and what do they say about acting on them?

## Method

Desk research: William Blair *Consumer-Centric Healthcare 2026*, citing Experian Health *2025 State of Patient Access* (survey of 213 healthcare employees and 1,004 patients); Robert Wood Johnson Foundation data as reported by William Blair.

## Findings

- **Finding.** Estimate receipt is rising while perceived accuracy is falling.
  - Evidence: 41% received an estimate in 2025; 71% said it was accurate to $400, down from 78% in 2022.
  - Confidence: medium (large survey, but secondary reporting).
- **Finding.** Patients say they would postpone care without an accurate estimate.
  - Evidence: 43% said they would likely postpone care without an accurate estimate.
  - Confidence: low for behavior; this is stated intent.
- **Finding.** Medical debt is widespread among patients who carry it.
  - Evidence: 73.6% of surveyed patients carried medical debt; 66.1% of those were in collections.
  - Confidence: medium for the surveyed population; not specific to imaging or HDHPs.

## Raw signal

> "43% indicate they will likely postpone care without an accurate estimate." — William Blair, citing Experian Health

## What this does NOT tell us

- Whether the 43% actually cancel or postpone.
- Whether the respondents are employer-sponsored HDHP members choosing imaging.
- Whether an estimate would change a booking decision.
- Whether estimates are accurate enough for a specific service.
