# Strategy

The four strategy elements from the Vision Board, elaborated. Per Pichler the vision endures;
**strategy changes as you learn** — so every claim here is an assumption carrying evidence.

> [!warning] The test
> If you cannot name the evidence behind a strategy element, write `Evidence unavailable.`
> A strategy with no falsifiable claims is a slogan.

# Strategy

## Example — US healthcare consumer

> [!example]- Example — transparent outpatient care
> **Target group:** Adults on employer-sponsored HDHPs choosing outpatient imaging.
> **Differentiator:** Upfront, accurate out-of-pocket cost estimate.
> **Priority thesis:** Reduce cost shock and avoid postponed care.
> **Falsifier:** If cost transparency does not reduce postponed care or unexpected bill rate, abandon.
>
> Sourced example, not product evidence.

> [!example]- Worked example — fictional
> **Priority thesis:** If we auto-offer shift gaps to eligible staff instead of requiring the
> manager to choose, then median time-to-fill drops below 15 minutes, because 9 of 11 interviewed
> managers describe the delay as decision latency, not staff shortage.
>
> **Differentiation**
> | Alternative | Does well | Fails our group | Our answer |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Deputy | Full workforce suite | Onboarding takes weeks for a 12-person clinic | Two-field setup, live in 10 minutes |
> | Spreadsheet + group chat | Free, familiar | No audit trail, no rest-period check | Keep the familiar feel, add the guardrail |
>
> **Not doing:** payroll, time-and-attendance, HR records. **Invalidated if** clinics say the
> delay is staffing shortage rather than decision latency.
